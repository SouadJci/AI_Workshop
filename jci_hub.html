Block 1 · The Method
The Prompt Formula
4 blocks. Works on any AI tool. Briefing AI = briefing a new team member on their first day.
1
ROLE — Who should AI be?
Sets vocabulary, depth, perspective. Without a role = generic output.
"You are a senior project coordinator with experience in youth leadership organizations..."
2
CONTEXT — What's the situation?
Most people skip this. Without context, AI invents context — and gets it wrong.
"JCI Tétouan · INNOVACTION competition · June 14 · 11 teams registered, need 15..."
3
TASK — What exactly do you need?
Specific task = specific output. Vague task = vague output.
"Write a 150-word update email to the steering committee. Flag registration gap as priority."
4
FORMAT — How do you want it?
Length, tone, language, structure. Without format, AI chooses — usually wrong.
"Professional tone · English · Under 150 words · WhatsApp-friendly line breaks"
↺  ITERATE — stay in the same chat, refine with one follow-up

Pair exercise · 10 min

Find a partner. Pick one real task you do regularly. Build a 4-block prompt. Run it. Iterate once.
Your prompt template
ROLE: You are [expert role relevant to your task]... CONTEXT: [Your organization / project / situation / audience / constraints] TASK: [Exactly what you need — specific, not vague] FORMAT: [Tone · Language · Length · Structure]
After the output: try "Make it shorter" or "Give me 3 versions" in the same chat.
The quality of your output = the quality of your brief.
Every time AI disappoints you — look at your prompt first.
Simulation 01 · Teams of 3–4
Chaos to Clarity
Messy meeting notes → structured action plan + follow-up message. 3 prompts, 13 minutes.

Setup: One person on the AI. Everyone else reads the output and directs. Keep the same chat open for all 3 steps.

Your raw notes

ok so we started late, maybe 6:15. Amine said registration is going slower than expected — only 8 teams confirmed so far, we need 15 minimum. Fatima thinks we should extend the deadline but Mohamed disagrees, says it sends the wrong signal. No decision made on that. Sponsors — Youssef has been talking to 3 companies: a bank (name?), a local tech startup called NovaTech, and a supermarket chain. Bank needs a formal dossier by end of month. NovaTech said maybe. 01 Extract & Diagnose 4 min ▾
Paste the raw notes into AI. Get a clear diagnosis before building anything.
Prompt 1
You are a senior project coordinator with experience in youth organizations. I'm giving you raw, messy notes from a planning meeting. Extract what actually happened. [PASTE THE RAW NOTES HERE] Give me: 1. DECISIONS MADE — things clearly agreed upon 2. OPEN QUESTIONS — discussed but unresolved 3. TASKS MENTIONED — action items, even without owners 4. RED FLAGS — urgent or at-risk items 5. MISSING INFORMATION — gaps in names, dates, owners Bullet points. Don't add things not in the notes.
02 Build the Action Plan 5 min ▾
Same chat. AI already has context — don't start a new conversation.
Prompt 2 — same chat
Based on the analysis above, build a structured action plan for INNOVACTION. Table format: | Task | Owner | Deadline | Priority | Then add: — TOP 3 PRIORITIES for the next 7 days — CRITICAL BLOCKER — the one thing that puts the project at risk — NEXT MEETING AGENDA — 3 items with time allocations Missing owners → write "TBD — assign at next meeting" English. Direct, action-oriented tone.
03 Write the Follow-Up 4 min ▾
Still same chat. This is your real deliverable — the message that moves the project forward.
Prompt 3 — same chat
Using the action plan above, write a follow-up message for the full INNOVACTION team. Include: — Brief summary of what was accomplished (2 sentences max) — Key decisions made — Tasks with owners and deadlines — 1–2 things needing immediate attention — Next meeting date + call to confirm attendance Format: WhatsApp-friendly. Short paragraphs. Under 250 words. Tone: professional but warm — this is a volunteer team.
Iterate:
"Cut to 5 bullet points for WhatsApp." "Make the tone more energetic." "The critical blocker is the venue — rewrite priorities around that."

01 Prioritize & Strategize 4 min ▾
Feed the full situation. Get priority order, angles per sponsor, and the one quick win. Read it with your team — do you agree?
Prompt 1 — strategy first
You are a partnership consultant with 10 years helping youth organizations close sponsorships in Morocco. SITUATION: — Organization: JCI Tétouan, 45 members — Event: INNOVACTION, June 14, Tétouan — 200+ attendees, 15 teams, regional press — Budget: MAD 45,000 total · MAD 18,000 confirmed · MAD 27,000 still needed · 6 weeks left PROSPECTS: 1. Banque Populaire — warm, asked for dossier 3 weeks ago, haven't sent it yet 2. Groupe Elco (manufacturing) — met at JCI event 3 months ago, said "send something", we never did 3. Al Omrane (real estate) — cold, no prior relationship, sponsors similar events in Casablanca Give me: 1. PRIORITY ORDER — who to contact first, second, third (2-sentence rationale each) 2. STRATEGIC ANGLE — for each, why would they sponsor INNOVACTION? (from their perspective) 3. QUICK WIN — the single action that most improves our chances today Be direct. 6 weeks. We need decisions, not reassurance.
02 Craft Two Messages 6 min ▾
Same chat. Two different messages — read them aloud. If they sound the same, that's the problem to fix.
Prompt 2 — same chat, two messages
Write two outreach messages for INNOVACTION (JCI competition, June 14 Tétouan, 200+ attendees, regional press). MESSAGE 1 — Bank re-engagement: Recipient: CSR manager, Banque Populaire Situation: They asked for a dossier 3 weeks ago. We haven't sent it. Re-engage without over-apologizing. Their interest: visibility with young professionals — key banking demographic. Format: Formal email. English. Under 200 words. Specific next step with a date. MESSAGE 2 — Elco reactivation: Recipient: Groupe Elco (unknown contact) Situation: Met 3 months ago, they said "send something", we never did. Their interest: employer brand, community presence, regional visibility. Format: WhatsApp. Human, low-pressure. Under 100 words. Easy ask — not "please sponsor us." Make them feel genuinely different in tone and approach.
Bank:
Formal · Forward-moving · Specific date · No grovelling
Elco:
Human · Warm · Low-pressure · Easy to reply to
03 Build the Follow-Up Sequence 3 min ▾
Sponsorships close on the 3rd touchpoint, not the 1st. Build the system for the bank — your highest-priority prospect.
Prompt 3 — same chat
Design a 3-touch follow-up sequence for Banque Populaire — our highest-priority prospect. Context: — Sending the dossier today — Event: June 14 (6 weeks away) — Decision needed by June 1 (printing/signage deadline) — Channels: email (primary) + WhatsApp (secondary) For each touch: — Timing — Channel — Opening line or subject — Core message (2 sentences) — Specific ask Also: what if they go silent after Touch 3? One concrete recommendation. Keep it calendar-ready.
Bonus:
"Write the Al Omrane cold outreach — make it stand out." "Write a 60-second phone script for the bank follow-up." "Turn the sequence into a Notion checklist."
